# Steady: the three self-care tools in brief

Live demo: https://steady-self-care.netlify.app

Steady is three short self-care tools for students, each for a different moment: stress, overthinking and loneliness. Each one takes 1–3 minutes, works on a phone, needs no account, and stores nothing.

---

## 01 Breathe (stress and anxiety)

**What it is:** a guided breathing exercise with a circle that grows and shrinks.

**What it aims to do:** calm the body's stress response in a minute or two, so you can think straight again.

**How it does it:**
- Choose a rhythm:
  - **Gentle** (in 4, out 5)
  - **Calming sigh** (a breath in, a short extra sip of air, then a long, slow breath out)
  - **Box** (4-4-4-4)
- Choose 1, 2 or 3 minutes.
- Breathe along with the circle. A soft chime or vibration is optional.
- Sessions always finish at the end of a full breath, never mid-breath.
- Afterwards, a quick check of how you feel gets a reply that matches your answer, then one prompt: "What's one thing you can focus on next?"

**Research basis:**
- **Long breath out:** slow breathing where the breath out is longer than the breath in helps the body switch to its calm "rest and digest" mode, through the vagus nerve. Zaccaro et al., 2018, *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*, a systematic review.
- **Calming sigh:** in a Stanford study, five minutes a day of this "cyclic sighing" for a month improved mood and lowered breathing rate more than mindfulness meditation did. Balban et al., 2023, *Cell Reports Medicine*.
- **Gentle:** the simple in-and-out pattern follows the NHS breathing exercise for stress.
- **Box:** included because it's familiar and easy to follow. There's less direct evidence for it.

## 02 Untangle (overthinking)

**What it is:** a 6-step guided exercise for one thought that keeps going round in your head.

**What it aims to do:** help you step back from the thought, separate what's actually true from what you're guessing, and end with one small next step instead of more worrying.

**How it does it:**
1. **Write the thought down.** Example thoughts are there to help you start.
2. **Step back from it.** The app shows it back as *"I'm having the thought that…"*.
3. **Facts or guesses.** The app splits the thought into parts, and you mark each part as a fact or a guess.
4. **A friend's view.** What would you say to a friend who had this thought?
5. **A balanced thought.** Write a fairer version, with sentence starters to help.
6. **One next step.** Choose a small action and when you'll do it.

You end with a summary card you can copy.

**Research basis:**
- **Stepping back from the thought** (step 2) comes from "cognitive defusion" in Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT). In a lab study, defusion made negative thoughts about yourself feel less believable and less upsetting, and it did better than trying to argue with the thought. Larsson et al., 2016, *Behavior Modification*.
- **Facts vs guesses and the balanced thought** (steps 3 and 5) are the core of the "thought record" from Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT).
- **A friend's view** (step 4) uses self-distancing: looking at your problem as an outsider would makes it less emotional and easier to reason about. Kross & Ayduk's research.
- **Choosing a when** (step 6) is an "implementation intention", an if/when plan. These reliably help people follow through, with a medium-to-large effect (d ≈ 0.65) across 94 studies. Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006.

## 03 Reach Out (loneliness)

**What it is:** a 30-second moment of kindness to yourself, followed by a ready-made message you can send to someone.

**What it aims to do:** make loneliness easier to act on, by easing the shame first and then making the first message easy to send.

**How it does it:**
1. **A self-compassion moment** of three lines (you can skip it):
   - "This is hard right now."
   - "Lots of people feel this way, too."
   - "I can be kind to myself."
2. **Pick what you need:** someone to listen, to reconnect with a friend, or company.
3. **Get a message** you can edit, in a *casual* or *more open* tone.
4. **Send it:** copy it, or share it straight to WhatsApp or iMessage on a phone.

**Research basis:**
- **The three lines** follow the three parts of Kristin Neff's Self-Compassion Break: mindfulness, "common humanity" and self-kindness. For students, a brief self-compassion programme of about three weeks increased self-compassion and optimism and reduced rumination. Smeets, Neff et al., 2014, *Journal of Clinical Psychology*.
- **Self-compassion and loneliness:** a meta-analysis found people with more self-compassion tend to be much less lonely (r ≈ −0.55).
- **Changing how lonely people think:** across loneliness interventions, the ones that tackle how lonely people think about others work better than ones that only offer more chances to meet people. Masi et al., 2011, *Personality and Social Psychology Review*. That's why the kindness moment comes before the message.
- **Ready-made messages:** people underestimate how much others appreciate being reached out to, and that keeps them from sending the message. Liu et al., 2022, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*. Templates make the first step easy.

---

**Design principles across all three:** short (1–3 minutes), skippable, no account, and nothing stored or sent anywhere. The wording is calm and doesn't lecture. This is a self-help demo, not therapy or crisis support. The wording should be reviewed by a counsellor before real students use it.
